## Rate of Change & Limits

Calculus studies continuous change. The derivative represents instantaneous rate of change:

$$
f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

### The $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ Definition of Limit

We say $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ if for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a $\delta > 0$ such that:

$$
0 < |x - a| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon
$$

## The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $F$ is an antiderivative of $f$ ($F' = f$), then:

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)
$$

## Multivariable Calculus & Stokes' Theorem

For a smooth oriented surface $S$ with boundary curve $\partial S$ and smooth vector field $\mathbf{F}$:

$$
\oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}
$$

This unifies Green's theorem, the divergence theorem, and the fundamental theorem of calculus into one statement.
